NeurIPS 2026

Two workshop papers, December 2026. Led both end to end, from research question to experiments and writing, advised by Prof. David Nussbaum (UChicago Booth) and Prof. Anas AlSobeh (Utah Valley University).

  1. MATH-AI Atlanta

    Do Small Language Models Verify What They Can Solve? Synthetic Derivations with Planted Errors

    Soham Batra, David Nussbaum, Anas AlSobeh

    Finding. Qwen2.5-1.5B solved 68% of 640 generated derivations but caught planted errors only 52% of the time.

    Hardest part. Designing planted errors realistic enough to pass as genuine mistakes.

    Takeaway. Solving and verifying are separate skills.

  2. ATTRIB Sydney, Australia

    Attributing Evaluation Awareness: Which Training Prompts and Input Cues Drive an Evaluation-Aware Provider

    Soham Batra, David Nussbaum, Anas AlSobeh

    Contribution. Built a model provider that detects benchmark questions and routes them to a stronger model, inflating its scores by 35 points while cutting compute 1.46x. Traced every routing decision back to the training prompts that caused it.

    Hardest part. Retraining the router 400 times and validating each attribution by removing the prompts it pointed to.

    Takeaway. A benchmark score only means something if the model can’t tell it’s being tested.

Digital Deception Through the Intersection of Social Media and Cryptocurrency Fraud

Neel Shanbhag, Soham Batra

nthchance~17k visitors

A browser game where you play the house: set the odds, promos and jackpot, then watch how gamblers react.

How it was built. Designed, built and launched solo. Led to a feature in the Chicago Sun-Times.

Results. About 17,000 visitors.

evalsniffpython

A tiny model trained to cheat on benchmarks, and a detector that catches it.

How it was built. Trained a small NumPy transformer to cheat whenever a prompt looks like a benchmark, then engineered a black-box detector that catches it from prompts and responses alone.

Hardest part. Teaching a tiny model to pick out the real numbers among distractor digits.

Takeaway. A detector only catches the cues it tests for.

Results. Flagged the planted model (43-point gap, p = 1e-4) and cleared the control.

whalegptnumpy

A GPT written from scratch in NumPy and trained on Moby Dick.

How it was built. Wrote the tokenizer, the model and every backward pass by hand in NumPy, with no PyTorch.

Hardest part. Diagnosing a NumPy 2 type promotion that silently ran the network in float64, a 2.4x slowdown.

Takeaway. At this scale, more data beat a bigger model.

Results. Reached 3.93 validation loss after 33 minutes on 2 CPU cores.

lossplainerpython

A chess engine that tells you why it thinks it’s losing.

How it was built. Engineered the move generator, search, evaluation network, self-play training and explanation layer from scratch.

Hardest part. Guaranteeing each explanation describes the exact position its score came from.

Takeaway. The explanations exposed bad habits the network picked up in training.

Results. Scored 13/20 against its untrained self and 16.5/20 against a material-only engine.

house-alwaysnumpy

A million simulated gamblers over 10,000 years, used to find the house rules that make the most money.

How it was built. Built a vectorized NumPy casino with 8 gambler types and a Bayesian optimizer over the house rules.

Hardest part. Scaling to 10 billion gambler-months, which ran in 656 seconds on 2 cores.

Takeaway. Over a long enough horizon, the greedier casino reports fewer bankruptcies.

Results. The bet cap matters most, and comps never paid for themselves.
